| M-06 | **The hook also fires on repeated failure, and text is also given word by word** (2026-11-18). Found in the first run: with every action proposed indifferently, Soar always has an operator, so the starting agent is never stuck (0 stuck steps in 100 episodes), and a hook on stuck alone would never fire: the agent would never learn. So the hook (`metasoar/hook.py`) fires on **stuck** (no action within `patience` decisions) and on **failing** (a level family failing at least half its training episodes, seeds 2000 and up, apart from the judge's above 10000). It writes a report and renders the request Substrate gets: intent, how it will be judged (from the base), what may change (`agent/**`), the card it must carry, and what was seen. And Soar rules match whole symbols, so a mission like "go to the red ball" was unusable as one string: the adapter now also gives any text of more than one word as `^ATTR-words`, one `^word` (`^text`, `^index`) each: a generic encoding of a Text value, not knowledge of any game. First report (20 training episodes a level): success 0.05, 0.2, 0.1, 0.3, 0.0; all five levels failing. | decided |

| M-12 | **ARC-AGI-3: perception names the scene, the rules learn a map of states by playing; passed by the judge** (2026-11-19; owner: "continue on the path of general perception and learning"). The policy's scope took `agent/perception/**/*.py` alone first, in #3. **Perception**, for any game: `state`, a signature of the frame leaving out ticking cells (a cell ticks when it changes on a step where at most two cells change besides those the moved objects explain; its region is masked from then on, and objects wholly inside the mask are not reported); `from`, `did`, `act`, `lost` name the last step; `mover`, the kind (colour and size) the plain actions move most often, with `at`, `was-at`, and `reset` when a move sends it back to where the attempt began by a jump that is not the action's usual step; each object's point is its own cell nearest its centroid; regions from runs and union-find with numpy (identical objects to before, about twice as fast); only fields the rules read go on the input link. **Rules** (`agent/explore.soar`): a node per state, holding what each action did there (tried, dead, fatal, where it led); for the level, points that did nothing or were clicked, places where a move hit a wall or a trap (`blocked`), how the mover moves and where it has been.
