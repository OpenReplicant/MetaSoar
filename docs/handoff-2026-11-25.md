## Decided today (all `proposed` in `DECISIONS.md`)

- **M-21:** System 1 skills have four kinds of handler (elaborator, proposer, applier, compiler) and a runner that
  owns the semantics (cache, calibrate scores to precision, decide by Soar's preferences, gate by side-effect
  class). The blackboard is shared, namespaced by skill, with declared and pinned imports. Lifecycle: proposed,
  shadow, active, with an audit rate. The active set is `system1.lock`, written by the judge. OpenEnv's rubric is the
  score of a case, one part of a Morphogen cell.
- **M-22:** matching and learning are case-based reasoning; a cascade by cost; typed questions with swappable
  backends (a local model read by its probabilities, Jev, a Hugging Face decision model); goals in the state,
  constraints as queries; `expects:` on every policy; typed impasses; three compilers (from the trace, near-miss,
  anti-unification); templates as the `fill` applier. Research note: `docs/typed-questions.md`.
- **M-23:** the pattern catalog, its own research line: `docs/pattern-catalog.md`.
- **M-24:** Soar-derived, not Soar orthodox; the Common Model of Cognition as the wider bar; departures listed
  (`docs/system1-skills.md`, "Relation to Soar"); impasses renamed (`blocked`) and added (`apply-failure`); the
  experiments registered in three tiers: `docs/experiments.md`.

The owner's finding, recorded in M-22: a 1.7B local model read by its token probabilities "outperformed the

## Next, in order

### 1. E-04: when to think (`trials/when-to-think/`)

The first gate: cheap, labels made locally, and its data also gives E-06.

- **Questions** (`questions/*.jsonl`): `id`, `source`, `question`, `answer`, and for multiple choice `options`. Public
  benchmarks with known answers: reasoning and maths (GSM8K, MATH, ARC-Challenge), law or other reading-heavy sets,
  plain lookups as the contrast. Start from the questions the owner already tested the 1.7B on.
- **Labelling** (`label.py`): each question through the `slow` model (Inkling 2 Mini), thinking on and thinking off,
  through the gateway. Record each answer, whether it is right, tokens and seconds. The label: `helped` (on right,
  off wrong), `hurt` (off right, on wrong), `same`. Keep the raw outputs.
- **Observers**, each answering "should this input be processed with thinking?" with a probability:
  always-on; always-off; nearest neighbours on the questions' embeddings; logistic regression on them; the `fast`
  model's typed question; the 1.7B's typed question, read raw and debiased (both label orders, averaged); a Jev
  clone if one is installed. The typed-question readout: state and question, options `yes`/`no`, the answer
  position prefilled, thinking off, softmax over the two answers' tokens only. On a sample, check the read against
  the generated answer (`docs/typed-questions.md`, the first-token mismatch).
- **Evaluate** (`evaluate.py`): split train and held out by a hash of the id. Calibrate each observer on train only.
  On held out: accuracy against tokens and seconds as the threshold moves; Brier score; calibration error;
  order-flip rate for the typed questions; coverage at 5% risk. Report accuracy and calibration separately, so a win
  says which.
- **E-06 from the same data:** do the nearest training questions' labels predict a held-out question's label better
  than the base rate?
